# TBLAB-001: Sputum Specimen Collection, Transport and Storage

> PRACTICE DOCUMENT. Written for developing and testing this tool only.
> It describes a generic laboratory workflow and is not official, validated or clinical guidance.

## 1. Purpose and scope

This SOP describes how sputum specimens are collected, labelled, transported and stored for molecular testing for tuberculosis (TB). It applies to specimens tested with the rapid molecular assay in TBLAB-002 and with targeted sequencing in TBLAB-003.

## 2. Specimen collection

Collect sputum in a sterile, leak-proof, screw-cap container. Collection should take place in a well-ventilated area or outdoors, away from other people. A good specimen is at least 1 ml of sputum. Saliva or nasal secretions are not acceptable, because they reduce the chance of detecting TB. An early-morning specimen usually contains more bacteria than a specimen collected later in the day.

## 3. Labelling and request form

Label the container itself, not the lid, with the patient identifier, the date of collection and the specimen type. Each specimen must be accompanied by a completed request form. Specimens that are unlabelled or that do not match the request form must be rejected.

## 4. Transport

Transport specimens to the laboratory in a sealed secondary container with absorbent material. Keep specimens cool during transport, at 2–8 °C where possible. Specimens should reach the laboratory within 3 days of collection.

## 5. Storage before testing

If testing cannot start on the day of receipt, store specimens at 2–8 °C. Specimens for the rapid molecular assay may be stored for up to 10 days at 2–8 °C before testing. Do not freeze specimens intended for the rapid molecular assay.

## 6. Specimen rejection criteria

Reject a specimen and request a new one if any of the following applies:

- The container is leaking or broken.
- The specimen volume is less than 1 ml.
- The specimen is unlabelled or the label does not match the request form.
- The specimen is older than the maximum storage time in section 5.

## 7. Safety

Open specimen containers only inside a biosafety cabinet. Wear a laboratory coat, gloves and a respirator when handling sputum. Decontaminate work surfaces with an approved mycobactericidal disinfectant after each session.
